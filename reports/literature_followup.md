# A few follow-up papers, before calling a question “open”

Checked on **26 October 2026**. This is a selected-source reading guide, not a comprehensive review of the field through 2026. The primary manuscripts below were checked for the specific statements recorded here while preparing this package; their calculations and experiments were not reproduced. First-person passages are draft reading prompts for revision, not a record that the student has finished these papers.

The distinction I want to keep: **Gali–Maze's 2013 calculation did not directly treat dynamic Jahn–Teller motion**. This does not mean later researchers left that physics untreated. The following sources give me a route beyond that limitation.

## 1. Hepp et al.: connect level labels to measured spectra

Christian Hepp et al., *Electronic Structure of the Silicon Vacancy Color Center in Diamond*, **Physical Review Letters 112, 036405 (2014)**. [Published DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.112.036405). Read version: [arXiv:1310.3106v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1310.3106v1), submitted 11 October 2013, five PDF pages; the preprint title begins with “The”.

**What it adds:** magnetic-field and polarization measurements are compared with a group-theory model of the ground and excited states. The Zeeman pattern and model connect the spin/orbital assignments to observable optical lines (PDF pp. 2–4; Figs. 2–4; Eq. 1). This is a useful next reading for understanding what a small Hamiltonian can explain.

**My caution:** this preprint is near-contemporary with Gali–Maze, although the journal publication is in 2014. Its effective model includes fitted spin-orbit and JT-like parameters. I should not equate every fitted term with a unique microscopic mechanism, or mistake this for a direct reproduction of the 2013 DFT study.

## 2. Rogers et al.: experimental checks of symmetry and vibronic effects

Lachlan J. Rogers et al., *Electronic structure of the negatively charged silicon-vacancy center in diamond*, **Physical Review B 89, 235101 (2014)**. [Published DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.89.235101). Read version: [arXiv:1310.3131v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1310.3131v2), revised 4 March 2014, eight PDF pages; the preprint hyphenates “negatively-charged”.

**What it adds:** optical polarization measurements support the ⟨111⟩ split-vacancy orientation and, with the excitation argument, D3d symmetry (Secs. II–III; Figs. 2–7). Fine-structure polarization supports a spin-orbit origin of the splitting, while phonon-sideband polarization provides evidence for dynamic Jahn–Teller effects (Sec. IV; Fig. 8, PDF pp. 6–7). That gives me an example of different measurements probing different parts of an electronic model.

**My caution:** evidence for dynamic JT does not amount to solving every relaxation process. Nor should I automatically identify all electronic levels proposed in different papers just because their symmetry labels agree; configurations and energies also matter.

## 3. Thiering and Gali: treat spin, orbit, and vibrations together

Gergő Thiering and Adam Gali, *Ab Initio Magneto-Optical Spectrum of Group-IV Vacancy Color Centers in Diamond*, **Physical Review X 8, 021063 (2018)**. [Published DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.8.021063). Read version: [arXiv:1804.07004v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.07004v1), submitted 19 April 2018, sixteen PDF pages.

**What it adds:** the methods explicitly introduce a dynamic JT Hamiltonian and its coupling to spin-orbit physics. They discuss orbital-moment reduction and compare a reduced treatment with joint diagonalization (Sec. II, PDF pp. 2–3, Eq. 4; Sec. III; Table III, PDF p. 9). This directly extends the static-method boundary we noticed in 2013.

**My caution:** this is a more advanced calculation, not what our two-state parity exercise implements. The authors also revisit the interpretation of a JT-like term in earlier effective models as residual strain (PDF p. 9). I need to compare definitions before comparing parameters across papers.

**Correction to read with it:** the authors' [2020 erratum, Physical Review X 10, 039901](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.10.039901) corrects the lifetime formula, some table entries/references, and effective-Hamiltonian expressions and signs. They state that the numerical results and conclusions are unchanged. Before implementing that Hamiltonian, use the corrected equations and units in the [four-page erratum](https://journals.aps.org/prx/pdf/10.1103/PhysRevX.10.039901), rather than copying the 2018 preprint alone. None of our three exercises uses those corrected equations.
